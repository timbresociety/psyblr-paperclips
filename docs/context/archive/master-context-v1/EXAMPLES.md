# SoloUnicorn representative examples

These examples communicate intended decision shape and tone. Engine values come from `engine/`; copy here is not a hidden balance rule.

## Demand: qualified attention

**Signal: “A crowded vertical is suddenly buying workflow software.”** The player swipes toward Pursue, passes or spends more to chase it. The result is a segment-specific qualified opportunity with acquisition cost and expiry. Impressions are not ARR.

## Product: semantic shipping

**Request: “A billing workflow with reliable identity and an audit trail.”** The player assembles relevant components, chooses verification depth and decides whether to ship early. A rushed release activates sooner while increasing defect/incident exposure and downstream work.

## Monetisation: price fit

A moving band shows the segment's willingness-to-pay and the posted monthly price. The player commits after reading fit. A success creates a paying subscription, not immediate cash. Billing and collection happen later.

## Retention: prioritize a threat

Several accounts approach churn. The player chooses based on ARR at risk, deadline, cause and save probability. A successful intervention removes the pending loss. It does not book the account's ARR a second time.

## Expansion: fit the package

An eligible account needs Security, Analytics and Automation. The player merges a package that fits the account. A successful addon consumes one of two account slots and raises MRR plus service cost. An ineligible or delinquent account cannot be sold an expansion.

## Operations: investigate before gambling

An incident reveals a retry storm, cloud bill spike or recursive workflow. Scratching exposes evidence and resolution options. **Black Box Optimizer** can offer a saving, no change or a new incident with explicit probabilities and cash/rot stakes. It is a trap-like option, not a guaranteed reward or mandatory chore.

## Finance: spend versus runway

The founder can buy a Product rank, fund a Retention agent or preserve cash for a scheduled debt payment. The Finance surface shows cash remaining, recurring upkeep, forecast shortfall, debt schedule, ownership and accepted VC terms. It does not create ARR.

## Founder relic examples for later evaluation

- **Actually Read the Diff:** verified Product work takes longer; defect escape probability falls.
- **Inbox Archaeologist:** Retention threats reveal their cause earlier; monitoring adds compute/upkeep.
- **Rainy-Day Rule:** automated spending respects a founder-set cash reserve; useful purchases may be delayed.
- **The Cloud Receipt:** retry-cost attribution enables selective throttling; throttled work completes more slowly.

These remain proposals. Each shipped relic needs an effect ID, exact value, eligibility reason, downside and repeat-founder reporting. Failed runs do not award new relic power; successful founders retain their advantages.

## Strategy examples

- **Reliability Quarter:** slower Product release throughput, lower defect/incident exposure.
- **Self-Serve Everything:** lower founder attention per sale, weaker enterprise fit and different Retention pressure.
- **Enterprise Upmarket:** higher possible ARR per account, slower close and higher service burden.
- **Default Alive:** lower cash burn and narrower near-term growth capacity.

Product-Led Machine, Distribution Goblin, NRR Fortress, Default-Alive Bootstrapper, Leveraged Blitz, VC Hypergrowth, Operations Fortress and Variance Goblin should be inferred from actual state. A label never changes the economy by itself.
