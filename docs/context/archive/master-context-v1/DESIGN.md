# SoloUnicorn visual, interaction, motion and audio contract

**Status:** canonical presentation guidance, revision `1.0-engine-consolidated`. Product and engine rules are in `CONTEXT.md` and `engine/`. The original v4 visual language is retained here and reconciled with continuous live simulation.

Authority: art direction and presentation quality. Reference bytes and interpretation are in [references/REFERENCE_ATLAS.md](references/REFERENCE_ATLAS.md).

## 1. The intended feel

Minimal, luxurious UI around rich tactile game objects. Confident typography, clear financial instrumentation and purposeful interaction should make a small founder feel capable of operating something improbably powerful.

The inherited visual composition is a dark graphite operational world with occasional pastel ethereal brand/milestone worlds. “Machine” is a material/interaction reference, not permission to build a generic terminal, sci-fi dashboard or wall of panels. “Sky” is aspiration and scale, not a busy painting behind small labels.

Use Paula Scher/Pentagram as a standard for identity and hierarchy, Monotype for typographic systems and numbers, and Gleb/Milkinside for integrated product, motion and physical response. These names do not substitute for concrete design decisions or authorize copying a recognizable execution.

## 2. Identity and exact references

Preserve the Structure primary mark and Spark secondary mark from the included images. Do not improvise a similar monogram or replace Spark with a stock sparkle. Material rendering may change surface appearance, never the defining silhouette or negative space.

The package contains raster reference geometry, not a verified vector master. A vector reconstruction needs overlay comparison at large and small sizes before adoption. Do not claim a production logo master is present.

Preserve the 1:1 app-icon composition. Wordmark and type roles must work independently from material effects. A mark should remain recognizable in a simple monochrome use.

## 4. Materials, objects and icons

Utility icons and work objects use materials that explain their meaning: optical glass, rubber, paper, metal, fabric, plastic or a coherent combination. Family consistency comes from camera, lighting, realism and silhouette, not coating everything in gold or liquid metal.

Visible semantic icons must be custom contextual hyperrealistic 3D image objects with transparent backgrounds and baked dimensionality. “Outlined” means clear silhouette/edge separation, not a cartoon stroke. No emoji, stock flat icons or generic premade 3D family as final product language.

Creation process:
product meaning → culturally resonant object → durable/nostalgic object → one original coherent fusion → premium art direction → small-size test.

Use one dominant silhouette and few supporting forms. No text baked into an ordinary icon, no opaque tile/card background, no unnecessary microscopic detail. The UI supplies the label, focus, state and hit area.

Rarity-bearing objects may evolve materially. A source example goes charcoal stone → gold → liquid glass → Ethereal. Lower tiers and tier count follow the asset's semantic journey. Ethereal is the apex: physically convincing iridescent liquid metal, with form-preserving dark reflection and controlled spectral color. It is not glass, foil, glitter or a CSS rainbow.

The reference card's APY text, names and layout are outside this game's canon. Use it to inspect material progression only.

## 5. Composition and attention

Keep a stable conceptual perimeter:
- top: score, ARR, cash, time and essential survival state;
- navigation: work functions and compact queue/agent state;
- context edge: obligations, selected details and build state;
- center: the current tactile object or player-opened management decision.

Do not wrap every region in an equally weighted rounded card. Keep the HUD calmer than the work object.

The alert inbox has a persistent known home. In mobile portrait it may move to a compact rail/edge while preserving discoverability. “Central inbox” means one understood aggregation point; it does not authorize an overlay on the center of an active gesture.

Urgency escalation should be noticeable: state/count, localized pulse, more vigorous motion/contrast, then a clear sustained critical cue. Coalesce repeated causes. Acknowledgement reduces sensory repetition; it does not erase the obligation.

No routine event may steal focus, change rooms or cover active play with a modal. Results, explicit paused states and deliberate player-opened objects can occupy the center. Finance, metric inspection and post-quarter choices continue the simulation unless the player explicitly pauses.

## 6. Tactile feedback and motion

Every major action has visible anticipation, commitment, response, resolution and return of control. Use:
- Demand: resisted swipe, accepted/rejected trajectory, demand entering the next queue.
- Product: component fit, assembly snap, verified state, shipping transfer or fault.
- Monetisation: visible band/cursor, decisive contact, priced customer moving into revenue.
- Retention: aimed intervention, threat impact, exposed ARR clearly protected or lost.
- Expansion: merge mass/shape change, package fit, account upgrade.
- Operations: scratch reveals evidence; resolution changes the relevant fault/cost, not generic coins.
- Finance: money allocated, obligation or ownership change made legible. No decorative reflex test.

Starting timing ranges from earlier design: microresponse 80–140ms, local state 180–260ms, panel 220–320ms, major reward 500–900ms. Tune for the actual gesture. These describe animation, not permissible input latency.

Keep one defining emotional beat per major flow: first agent visibly doing your work; a trusted workflow misrouting; a relic changing a rule; the unicorn threshold opening a glimpse of the ethereal world. Do not replay a cinematic for every completion.

Reserve longer choreography for safe transitions. Input acceptance must not wait for ornamental animation. Reduced motion keeps the same meaning through state and contrast.

## 7. Sound, music and haptics

Audio is part of the game specification, not a finishing checkbox.
- Ambient operating texture: low priority.
- UI tactility: concise press/snap/contact.
- Gameplay events: distinct physical sounds by action.
- Alerts: recognizable motifs and controlled escalation.
- Music/milestones: layered pacing and emotional transitions.

Prefer tactile contacts, precision clicks, magnetic snaps, short compression and restrained financial ticks. Ethereal shimmer is rare and contextual. Avoid generic casino jingles, constant alarm loops or fantasy sparkle on routine actions.

Music may add/remove rhythm and tension layers with throughput and pressure. Acknowledgement stops repeated alert audio. Aggregate repeated jobs, duck secondary sounds and preserve important cues. Silence or reduced density can emphasize a rare moment.

Expose independent SFX/music controls, mute, reduced motion and intensity options. Audio/haptics never carry essential information alone. Do not alter economics when an effect is disabled.

## 8. Mobile and readability

Mobile is a new composition, not scaled desktop. The active gesture needs useful area and thumb reach. Compact secondary state into predictable navigation and panels. Do not show six miniature workspaces at once or force landscape.

Use practical targets around 44 CSS pixels or larger where feasible, safe areas, stable pointer mapping and readable text expansion. Verify actual contrast under the rendered material, not just token pairs. The mobile Finance surface must reveal cost and consequences without cramped tables or precision tapping.

## 9. Quality evidence

Review the actual loop, not only a screenshot. Reject a design that is generic, visually rich but tactically unreadable, motion-heavy but causally unclear, or beautiful on desktop and unusable on touch.

The prior 20-point rubric can be a review aid. A self-awarded 17/20 is not owner approval and is not proof of quality. Require evidence of:
- clear action and result;
- original brand recall;
- material/type coherence;
- actual gesture quality;
- purposeful motion/audio;
- readable pressure states;
- first-class mobile composition;
- accessible alternatives and reduced motion;
- no generic final placeholders.

Inspect normal, successful, failed, overloaded and milestone states. Distinguish functional prototype, art-complete build and owner-accepted direction.
