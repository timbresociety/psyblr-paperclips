# Taste and game-sense contract

This document teaches judgment, not a visual recipe. Use it when a task can be technically correct yet still feel generic, confusing, over-designed, or unlike SoloUnicorn.

## 1. Product taste in one sentence

SoloUnicorn should feel like operating a beautiful, increasingly dangerous machine: calm, legible instrumentation around one tactile work object, with pressure emerging from meaningful business trade-offs rather than UI noise.

The player should feel competent before they feel overwhelmed.

## 2. The two visual planes: MACHINE + SKY

### MACHINE

Most active gameplay lives here.

- near-black graphite environment;
- precise, restrained instrumentation;
- physical or dimensional work objects;
- strong state feedback;
- compressed information only where pressure justifies it;
- calm high-contrast surfaces for financial truth;
- stable peripheral frame so attention changes are meaningful.

MACHINE is not a crypto terminal, hacker UI, sci-fi HUD, enterprise dashboard, spreadsheet, or wall of glass cards.

### SKY

Use for aspiration, milestone, rarity, transition, and emotional release.

- pastel ethereal worlds;
- pale cyan, lavender, blush and reflective depth;
- fog, monumental scale, softness and air;
- iridescence as scarce reward salience.

Do not smear SKY across ordinary navigation or dense gameplay. The contrast is valuable because the planes remain distinct. Merge them only at emotionally important transitions.

## 3. Hierarchy rules

A good screen answers these in under two seconds:

1. What am I working on right now?
2. What decision can I make?
3. What will it cost or risk?
4. What changed because of my last action?
5. What is becoming urgent elsewhere?

The center owns the current work object. Persistent company state stays at the perimeter. Urgency can move perceptually closer to the center through motion, sound, contrast, or inbox escalation, but routine information should not invade the work surface.

Do not make every object a rounded rectangle. Cards are containers, not a design language.

## 4. Luxury means restraint, not decoration

Prefer:

- fewer, better-defined objects;
- deliberate spacing;
- confident typography;
- material contrast;
- physically plausible depth;
- meaningful motion;
- strong silhouettes;
- quiet surfaces around one high-salience moment.

Reject:

- glass blur everywhere;
- generic gradients;
- rainbow iridescence on ordinary controls;
- neon outlines as default state;
- tiny labels used to signal sophistication;
- decorative charts;
- generic AI sparkles;
- generic crypto/Web3 visual language;
- fake sci-fi panels;
- stock emoji or flat icon packs as final assets;
- literal unicorn imagery unless a specific joke or milestone earns it.

If removing an effect makes the decision clearer without reducing meaning, remove it.

## 5. Icon and object taste

Final semantic icons are contextual 3D objects with transparent backgrounds, coherent camera/lighting and strong silhouettes. They should mix familiar physical culture with digital meaning when that increases recall.

An icon should communicate the concept before the label is read. Avoid forcing rarity materials onto ordinary semantic icons.

Use supplied references for dimensionality, material behavior and mood. Do not copy third-party compositions or branded objects.

If the required custom asset does not exist, use a clean temporary placeholder and create an explicit asset request. Do not permanently solve a bespoke 3D asset requirement with a generic icon library.

## 6. Interaction taste

Every interaction must justify being tactile.

A good tactile interaction has:

```text
readable question
→ physical intention
→ resistance / commitment threshold
→ immediate local response
→ deterministic mechanical result
→ visible downstream consequence
→ next attention choice
```

The gesture should be close enough to the work metaphor that the player can explain it without lore.

Good examples in this product:

- triage something by swiping it away or committing it;
- assemble a product from meaningful components;
- time a pricing commitment against visible willingness-to-pay;
- aim attention at a threatened customer;
- merge account needs into an expansion package;
- scratch/reveal operational evidence before accepting risk.

Bad interaction patterns:

- a button masquerading as a minigame;
- drag-and-drop with arbitrary destinations;
- interaction complexity that does not add a decision;
- random timing with no readable model;
- celebratory particles obscuring causal state;
- gestures that feel satisfying but have no strategic consequence;
- failure whose cause becomes visible only after the penalty.

## 7. Game sense: what makes a mechanic earn its place

A mechanic should create at least two of the following, and ideally several:

- a meaningful decision;
- mastery;
- risk management;
- tempo pressure;
- resource trade-off;
- build expression;
- information trade-off;
- opportunity cost;
- a downstream consequence the player can anticipate.

A new mechanic that merely adds another meter, currency, button, screen, or animation does not earn scope.

Ask: **what existing decision becomes richer because this exists?** If the answer is unclear, cut or defer it.

## 8. Founder attention is the central scarce resource

Continuous time matters because the founder cannot do everything simultaneously. The game should create pressure through neglected queues, deadlines, cash timing, threats, obligations and overextension.

Do not create pressure primarily by increasing visual chaos.

A strong moment is:

> “I know Retention is deteriorating, but this Product opportunity expires in eight seconds and I need cash next month.”

A weak moment is:

> “Five red badges are flashing and I cannot tell which one matters.”

Attention switching should feel costly because something else continues to happen, not because navigation itself is annoying.

## 9. Anticipation before punishment

Show the player enough information to make an informed bet:

- deadlines;
- queue expiry;
- exposed ARR;
- likely consequence;
- VC mandate requirement;
- debt obligation;
- operational risk distribution where appropriate.

Uncertainty is desirable. Hidden causality is not.

The player should be able to say, “I knew this could happen and chose the risk,” rather than “the game randomly punished me.”

## 10. Stability is a reward

Do not make every second high intensity. A well-built company should occasionally feel calm because the player earned control through automation, capacity, good strategy, or strong systems.

Then new scale can destabilize it again.

The emotional rhythm should include:

```text
manual friction
→ competence
→ relief
→ growth
→ new bottleneck
→ overextension
→ recovery / redesign
→ temporary mastery
```

Constant red alert destroys the contrast required for pressure to feel meaningful.

## 11. Roguelike sense

Strong upgrades should change decisions, not simply increase numbers.

Prefer combinations that create emergent strategies across systems. Avoid one universally dominant build.

Relics and permanent choices should be legible enough that a player can form a plan, but open enough for surprising combinations. A high-variance option should usually have understandable positive expected value while increasing mayhem or downside exposure.

Repeat founders may legitimately become stronger through founder-history unlocks. Meta progression does not need to preserve identical difficulty for new and experienced players; it should create alternate builds and earned leverage without deleting the company simulation.

## 12. Business simulation truth

Do not collapse distinct concepts because the UI would be easier:

- ARR is not cash;
- signed contract is not collection;
- demand is not activation;
- activation is not monetisation;
- strain is not complexity;
- complexity is not context rot;
- operations capacity is not throughput everywhere;
- valuation is score, not a substitute for operating state.

A player should be able to trace major value changes causally.

## 13. Copy taste

Copy should sound human, culturally aware and specific. Humor comes from recognition of founder/AI/startup behavior, not from random meme density.

Prefer a sharp object or situation over generic startup jargon. References may be contemporary, Indian or global when the joke survives without requiring explanation. Avoid direct copying of third-party creative expression.

Do not let witty copy hide mechanical meaning. The mechanical consequence must remain obvious.

## 14. Mobile and desktop are compositions, not breakpoints

Desktop can use peripheral instrumentation around a large center. Mobile should preserve the same causal model but recompose hierarchy for thumb reach and limited width.

Never solve mobile by shrinking the desktop cockpit until everything fits. Preserve:

1. current work object;
2. decision-critical state;
3. primary action;
4. urgent pressure;
5. only then secondary instrumentation.

## 15. Taste-killing failure modes

Before calling visible work complete, actively search for:

- generic SaaS dashboard composition;
- center dominated by text instead of an object;
- too many equal-weight panels;
- excessive glass, glow, gradients or iridescence;
- no quiet areas;
- unclear causal feedback;
- meters with no actionable decision;
- “gameplay” that is actually form filling;
- arbitrary drag targets;
- unreadable pressure;
- punishment before warning;
- interaction animation that blocks input;
- mobile that looks like compressed desktop;
- beautiful presentation that lies about system state;
- a mechanic whose removal would not change a player's strategy.

## 16. Decision rule when taste is uncertain

Do not invent certainty from this document.

If two implementations both satisfy the rules and the difference is experiential, build the cheaper reversible version and test it in the running product.

The correct question is not “which one matches the canon perfectly?” It is “what is the cheapest artifact that lets us observe which one produces the intended player behavior?”

## 17. Reference lenses: what to learn, not what to copy

These references are diagnostic lenses. Borrow the underlying design/game principle, not proprietary visual expression, characters, copy, layouts, or assets.

### Identity / typography lens

From high-recall identity systems associated with studios/designers such as Paula Scher and Monotype, learn:

- recognizable structure before ornament;
- typography that carries personality through proportion, rhythm and hierarchy;
- a mark/system that remains identifiable when stripped of effects;
- confidence through consistency rather than maximal decoration.

Do not imitate a specific designer's signature style. Ask whether the system has comparable clarity, recall and typographic discipline.

### Product interaction / industrial-design lens

From the kind of work the user references through Gleb / Milkinside, learn:

- digital controls should feel intentionally shaped for human manipulation;
- motion exposes affordance, mass, resistance and state change;
- the interface can feel physical without skeuomorphic clutter;
- novel interaction must improve comprehension or emotional consequence, not exist as a portfolio trick.

Judge the interaction by whether the player understands what their hand is doing to the system.

### Game-design criticism lens

From sources such as Game Maker's Toolkit, Game Design Library and Indie Game Clinic, learn to ask:

- what decision is the player actually making?
- what information do they have before making it?
- what skill can improve over repeated runs?
- how does the game teach the rule through play?
- what is the dominant strategy and why?
- where does friction create depth versus merely waste time?
- what observable player behavior would falsify the design hypothesis?

Do not treat a coherent design explanation as evidence that the mechanic is fun. Build and observe.

### Mechanic reference lens

Use known references only to preserve the useful mechanic grammar:

- **Scritchy Scratchers / Lucky Cat:** Operations should preserve the feeling of voluntarily exposing a tempting high-variance optimization, not a guaranteed reward dispenser.
- **Gossip Harbor-style merge grammar:** merging is useful when object combination and scarcity make the next choice legible; do not import generic merge progression automatically.
- **How Many Dudes:** post-round choices and build-dependent pools are valuable because prior decisions change future option value; avoid detached random perk menus.
- **Bills Must Be Paid / satisfying hyper-casual loops:** a simple physical verb can carry a work function, but only if the business consequence remains visible.
- **Vibe-coding / one-person-AI-company culture:** cultural relevance creates recognition and comedy, but the game must remain mechanically interesting after the reference ages.

### Evaluation principle

When references conflict, prefer the principle that improves player comprehension, strategic choice and causal truth in the current SoloUnicorn build. References never override the product's own interaction logic.
